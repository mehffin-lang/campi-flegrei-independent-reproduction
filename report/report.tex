\begin{abstract}
Tan et al. analyze an observational earthquake catalog; they do not specify a coupled physical forward model. I retrieve original INGV waveform observations and station metadata, execute the explicitly permitted retrained PhaseNet model with independently written preprocessing, and calculate waveform and spectral displays for three templates specified in the supplementary methods. These calculations replace the previous synthetic-only presentation. Three supplementary panel groups are partially reproduced: S1, S2, and S15C. Two additional template figures are related observational analyses; their events differ from the published S15A/B examples. No main-manuscript panel is reproduced. The full association, relocation, magnitude calibration and focal-mechanism workflow remains incomplete. This report separates successful observational processing from missing inputs, restricted derived products, and unimplemented downstream analysis.
\end{abstract}

\section{Actual retrieval and coverage}
The INGV landing page failed local TLS issuer verification. The official \url{https://webservices.ingv.it/fdsnws/} station and dataselect endpoints succeeded with normal certificate verification. I requested channel metadata intersecting 21 January 2022--20 March 2025 within 40.7--40.95 degrees N and 13.9--14.3 degrees E, and complete response metadata for CAAM, COLB, CSOB and CSTH. Station epochs describe metadata validity, not guaranteed continuous observation coverage.

I retrieved a calendar-selected validation day, 1 January 2024, at those four stations in hourly three-component chunks. Its date was chosen independently of every event catalog. This is one calendar day within a 1155-day study interval, plus the separate template windows; it does not characterize full-period detection performance. I also retrieved three-minute CAAM/COLB windows around the three template times stated in supplementary S9: 6 May 2024 22:11:10, 8 March 2024 17:40:54 and 17 February 2025 08:06:48 UTC. These method-defined windows support template displays; they do not seed the calendar-day picker.

The temporary-station landing page is accessible. It describes an archive of recordings cut from five seconds before to 55 seconds after author-detected origin times at V0105, V0102, CRUS, CROS, CMDP, CFB1, CFB3, CREM, CSOC, CLAC and CGAU. Its HTTP headers report 3,293,243,207 bytes. Downloading that whole archive would not supply an unbiased continuous record: its selection already incorporates the authors' detection results. \section{Documented scientific workflow and remaining dependencies}
The paper describes retrained PhaseNet detection, GaMMA association, VELEST initial location and velocity calibration, TomoDD differential-time relocation, relative magnitudes, SKHASH/gCAP focal mechanisms and hybrid-template analysis. The methods specify 30-second picker windows, probability threshold 0.3, and exclusion of S picks at CASE, CFB1 and CFB3. Those stations are absent from this validation run. GaMMA requires at least six picks; VELEST applies residual cutoffs of 0.4 then 0.3 seconds and retains events with at least six picks, RMS at most 0.25 seconds and gap at most 300 degrees. TomoDD uses two rounds with picked and waveform-correlated differential times.

Association and relocation were not implemented in this run. The required initial 1D and fixed 3D velocity specifications are not supplied as permitted executable inputs here; the uploaded repository's fitted velocity products remain excluded. The written methods also leave damping, full iteration settings, exact calibration-event selection and correlation weighting incomplete. This is a combination of restricted inputs and unfinished implementation, not a claim that all seismic data are unavailable.

Absolute magnitude estimation presents an additional policy conflict: the paper regularizes against the INGV duration magnitudes, whereas the current instruction permits that catalog only as a final comparison. I do not import those magnitudes as priors. Relative amplitude differences alone leave a common magnitude offset undetermined. Focal mechanisms further require independent event locations, polarities, amplitude ratios and Green's functions. No synthetic volcano model is introduced to replace these missing analyses. Physical initial or boundary conditions are not applicable to the implemented signal-processing operations.

\section{Independent numerical processing}
\subsection{Waveforms and spectra}
ObsPy reads original miniSEED records and StationXML. I reject gaps in the short template streams, check finite samples and 100 Hz sampling, remove the segment mean and divide by the documented channel sensitivity to obtain velocity units. This is sensitivity correction, not full deconvolution of instrument response. The latter is not fully specified for the published display. This choice may affect amplitude and spectral agreement and is not concealed as an exact reproduction.

Each template display spans two seconds before to 18 seconds after its written method time. Each component is normalized by the corresponding CAAM component's maximum absolute amplitude within that window. COLB uses the same CAAM divisor, preserving interstation amplitude ratios. The written caption does not completely specify the normalization window, component pooling or display filter. I display demeaned, sensitivity-corrected signals without an additional bandpass. Spectrograms use 128-sample Hann windows, 112-sample overlap, constant detrending and one-sided power density at 100 Hz. These are explicit display assumptions, not inferred from published pixels.

The frequency index is supplementary equation S4:
\[
\mathrm{FI}=\log_{10}\left(\frac{P_{8\text{--}15\,\mathrm{Hz}}}{P_{2\text{--}5\,\mathrm{Hz}}}\right).
\]
I calculate it on an 800-sample CAAM vertical window beginning at each method-specified template time. A demeaned rectangular-window DFT gives one-sided Parseval-normalized power; inclusive frequency bins define each band. The exact onset alignment and spectral taper are incompletely specified, so the resulting values are independent measurements for the stated windows, not asserted matches to author event values. The separate 0.2--6 Hz template-matching filter must not be used for an 8--15 Hz FI estimate. An additional independent template search filters the written templates and calendar-day CAAM/COLB vertical traces at 0.2--6 Hz, computes normalized sliding correlations, and averages the two station scores. The scan uses the actual common recorded interval at the two stations, rather than assuming miniSEED blocks begin exactly at the requested hour. Candidates require a score above 0.25. A four-pole causal filter, 8-second minimum peak spacing and exclusion of 10-second chunk edges are explicit implementation choices. Template self-correlation must equal one; insertion at a known offset must recover that offset. Candidates are saved as unreviewed; they are not the published event list. No three-year template search or manual cluster review was completed.

\subsection{PhaseNet inference}
The explicitly authorized trained tensors are loaded with \texttt{weights\_only=True}; all state-dictionary keys match the installed SeisBench PhaseNet architecture. The supplied configuration defines ENZ input, 100 Hz sampling, standard-deviation normalization, 1500-sample overlap and 250-sample edge blinding. The installed architecture uses 3001 samples (30.01 seconds), corresponding to the nominal 30-second windows in the written method. The model README specifies a 1 Hz highpass. I use a four-pole causal Butterworth implementation after mean removal; pole count and phase convention are stated implementation assumptions. P and S thresholds are 0.3. Hourly streams are trimmed to requested intervals, split at gaps, and processed without interpolating missing observations. Identical station/phase/peak-time tuples are deduplicated. Window-edge blinding can miss arrivals near hourly boundaries; there is no completeness claim.

This is new inference using a permitted trained model, not independent training. The saved probabilities are model scores, not calibrated location uncertainties. Pick counts are not earthquake counts and cannot replace Figure 2's seismicity rates. No author picks or events enter preprocessing, thresholds or selection.

\section{Reproduction status and discrepancies}
No main-manuscript panel is reproduced. S1, S2 and S15C are partially reproduced; all other publication panel groups are not reproduced. The figure-by-figure record below retains every caption-defined main and supplementary group. The machine-readable \path{docs/panel_record.json} additionally records permitted input files and generation/plot commands. For the three partial groups and two related template examples, commands are \path{.venv/bin/python scripts/analyze_observations.py} and \path{.venv/bin/python scripts/plot_observations.py}. Groups with no numerical execution have null commands. The two related template examples retain their executed commands without claiming reproduction of the corresponding published panels. A written assessment is not counted as numerical execution.

The primary shortfall is the missing independent multi-year associated and relocated catalog. Main Figures 1A/C also involve historical catalog information allowed here only as a comparison reference; 1B needs displacement observations not supplied by the seismic services. S3 training examples and S4 original-versus-retrained examples are not reconstructed by applying the retrained model to a different validation day. S14 requires the complete template search and review; S15D's exact shallow example was not independently identified. Plot similarity is not substituted for these missing analyses.

Numerical station coordinates, waveform timing, spectra and FI are available in this run, but no event-matched numerical errors are calculated against prohibited author-derived result files. The report does not interpret absent errors as zero error. After generating the figures, visual comparison with S15 showed that the published A and B examples differ from the written-method E1/E2 template events used here. Their generated plots are therefore related examples, not panel reproductions. E3 corresponds to the S15C example; its dominant low-frequency coda is qualitatively similar, but filtering, normalization, alignment and spectral display differ. No pixel values were extracted or used to revise inputs. The independently generated spectra were compared visually only after generation; the published panels are not embedded in this report. The documented S7 letter inconsistency and S9 strict-versus-inclusive threshold ambiguity remain recorded in \path{docs/METHODS.md}; neither was silently used to filter an author catalog.

\section{Rebuild and replacement}
Create the local environment and retrieve inputs with the commands in the README. Run \path{.venv/bin/python scripts/rebuild.py --clean}. The rebuild verifies the four supplied files and downloaded-input hashes, checks readable captions before replacing outputs, compiles and runs the retained C++ consistency checks, executes observational processing and plotting, rebuilds tables, and compiles this report twice. Results and command exit codes are recorded separately from scientific completeness. Retrieval is a separate network step; the cached rebuild is offline.

The previous synthetic method-figure PDFs/PNGs and their plotting script were removed. Previous report source/PDF, panel records, build status and synthetic validation data were regenerated or replaced; the useful C++ validation implementation is retained and clearly labeled. New observational data and figures supersede the synthetic-only presentation. Exact changes are documented in \path{docs/REPLACEMENTS.md}. Uploaded source files, useful guidance, unrelated work and all Git history are preserved.

Inputs and environment reside in ignored \path{inputs/} and \path{.venv/}. Project intermediates and plotting/SeisBench caches reside in \path{tmp/}. An initial dependency import also created the standard SeisBench configuration cache at \path{/home/mehffin/.seisbench}; subsequent runs redirect it into the repository. Pip uses its standard user cache under \path{/home/mehffin/.cache/pip}; no scientific project results reside there. External files need not be committed: their retrieval procedure and checksums accompany the project. The full scientific deliverable remains incomplete despite a successful bounded rebuild.
