\begin{abstract}
Tan et al. develop an observational earthquake catalog and analyze its space--time distribution, magnitudes, focal mechanisms, and shallow hybrid events. The paper does not prescribe a coupled physical forward model. Both supplied PDFs are readable, including the supplementary methods and all figure captions. The uploaded archives contain derived catalogs, picks, focal solutions, and an interactive plot; the project restrictions exclude these as analytical inputs. I independently implement the documented relative-amplitude, signal-to-noise, and frequency-index equations and test a stated least-squares magnitude convention. Analytic consistency tests pass, but no empirical manuscript or supplementary panel is reproduced. This report identifies every figure and caption-defined panel or view, records its input blocker, and distinguishes generated method checks from observational reproduction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scientific premise and documented workflow}
The observational target is the 21 January 2022--20 March 2025 seismic sequence. The study uses 23 broadband stations, nine short-period stations, and 16 accelerometers (supplement S2). A retrained PhaseNet picker supplies phase probabilities; GaMMA associates arrivals; VELEST and TomoDD refine locations. Subsequent analyses estimate magnitudes, focal mechanisms, and hybrid-event properties. Discussions of pressure, fluid migration, and ring-fault behavior are interpretations of these observations, not a system of coupled evolution equations with specified forcing and boundary conditions.

The final retraining pools contain 24,547 local earthquake traces, 145,292 noise traces, and 102,620 STEAD traces. The methods specify 30-s windows, up to five overlapping events, mean-squared-error loss, 60 epochs, and learning rate 0.001. Detection uses a probability threshold of 0.3 and excludes S picks at CASE, CFB1, and CFB3. GaMMA requires at least six picks and yields 105,944 candidate events. These are documentary counts; none is recreated here.

VELEST calibration uses 373 selected events and two runs that refine the one-dimensional velocity model, locations, and then station corrections. Three further fixed-model runs relocate the catalog, with residual cutoffs of 0.4 s and 0.3 s. Final acceptance requires at least six picks, RMS at most 0.25 s, and azimuthal gap at most 300 degrees. The reported intermediate catalog has 61,809 events. Two TomoDD rounds use a fixed three-dimensional velocity model and both picked and cross-correlated differential times, yielding 54,319 events. The routine catalog has 12,083 events. Their count ratio is approximately 4.50, an arithmetic comparison of published totals rather than a measured detection gain from this implementation.

Table S1 supplies a layered velocity parameter model. However, the three-dimensional field, actual picks, waveforms, and selected event subsets needed for the relocation results are excluded original inputs. Retraining without the study's admissible observations would not recreate its network weights or event detections. The complete detection, association, and relocation workflow is therefore not implemented or claimed as validated. The implementation below is limited to explicitly documented analysis equations and their consistency tests.

\section{Independent implementation}
\subsection{Amplitude, power, and magnitude}
Supplementary equations S2 and S5 define a relative magnitude from positive amplitude ratios:
\begin{equation}
 d_{ij}=\log_{10}(A_i/A_j).
\end{equation}
Comparable propagation and station response are required for this difference to represent a magnitude difference. Equal amplitudes give zero; a common multiplicative station gain cancels. The absolute magnitude cannot be determined from these differences alone. Equation S1 includes attenuation and station corrections; its full coefficients and absolute calibration are not supplied as an independently executable specification here.

Equation S3 defines the signal-to-noise ratio as
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{SNR}=10\log_{10}(P_{\mathrm{signal}}/P_{\mathrm{noise}}).
\end{equation}
The written windows are 5 s after and 2 s before the actual or theoretical P arrival, with a 5 dB cutoff. I use mean-square power in each window. Integrated energy would introduce a spurious window-length ratio. The consistency test uses exact sinusoids with a factor-two amplitude difference, predicting $10\log_{10}4$ dB despite the unequal window lengths. No arrival time is inferred from a measured trace.

The magnitude methods describe a weighted least-squares inversion with duration-magnitude regularization, but not its complete objective or regularization strength. I test the explicit convention
\begin{equation}
 J(\mathbf m)=\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}(m_i-m_j-d_{ij})^2+
 \lambda\sum_i(m_i-p_i)^2,
\end{equation}
where $p_i$ are assumed prior magnitudes. This is a documented implementation choice, not a claim about the exact authors' objective. The small graph-Laplacian normal system is solved by Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting in C++. The authors describe gradient descent; the exact linear solve here tests a specified quadratic problem instead.

Four analytic test magnitudes are $[0,0.3,-0.2,0.8]$, with amplitudes $10^{m_i}$ and weights $w_{ij}=1/(j-i)$. With matching priors and $\lambda=1$, the solution should recover those magnitudes. A common prior shift must shift the solution equally. With $\lambda=0$, the global offset is undetermined, and the solver rejects the singular problem. A separate sensitivity calculation adds prior errors $[0.2,-0.1,0.1,-0.2]$ and varies $\lambda$ from 0.001 to 100. These arbitrary fixture values are not inferred from the publication and do not estimate its magnitude errors.

\subsection{Frequency index}
Supplementary equation S4 defines
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{FI}=\log_{10}(P_{8\text{--}15\,\mathrm{Hz}}/P_{2\text{--}5\,\mathrm{Hz}}).
\end{equation}
The paper specifies an 8-s CAAM waveform window and the two bands. Sampling, taper, spectral normalization, and endpoint conventions are not fully stated. For the consistency tests I choose 100 Hz sampling, a rectangular window, mean removal, and inclusive frequency-bin boundaries. A direct double-precision discrete Fourier transform gives one-sided powers, normalized by $N^2$ and doubled except at DC and Nyquist. The common normalization cancels in FI; Parseval's identity provides a separate power check.

For the analytic signal
\begin{equation}
 x(t)=\sin(2\pi\,3t)+r\sin(2\pi\,10t),\quad 0\leq t<8\ \mathrm{s},
\end{equation}
the two tones fall on exact Fourier bins, so $\mathrm{FI}=2\log_{10}r$. Their frequencies are test assumptions within the documented bands. The calculation does not generate an earthquake waveform. Common amplitude gain and circular time shifts must preserve FI. The 1--10 Hz display filter described for S14 is not imposed on the signal used to estimate the separate 8--15 Hz FI band.

There are no physical initial or boundary conditions: these are finite-window signal operations and a finite-dimensional inverse problem. No magma source, pressure history, fault law, or synthetic spatial earthquake distribution is imposed.

\section{Discrepancies in the written specification}
The uncertainty discussion on S5 refers to S7 panel letters H and I, whereas the figure and its caption use A--G: B/D/F give pick count versus error, and C/E/G give error densities. The panel record follows the figure and caption. Figure S9's methods retain events with NCCP+NCCS equal to 100, whereas its caption states a strict greater-than condition. Both predicates are recorded at 99, 100, and 101 in \texttt{data/thresholds.csv}; neither is applied to the prohibited catalog. The interactive plot has a different documented threshold, NCCP+NCCS+NCTP+NCTS greater than 1000, which must not be substituted for the S9 condition.

The focal-mechanism methods discuss 74 events, while the Data S3 caption describes 55 solutions. The archive was not mined to reconcile that difference. S15 uses the station spelling CCAM once, while the surrounding methods and normalization refer to CAAM. Remaining implementation gaps include exact magnitude-regularization strength, the cross-correlation weight convention, complete training splits/seeds and augmentation rejection rules, relocation damping and iteration settings, geographic polygons and time bins, event-dependent inversion bands, and kernel-density bandwidths. These are separate from the stronger excluded-input blocker.

\section{Comparison with the publication}
No empirical manuscript or supplementary panel is reproduced. No panel is classified as partially reproduced merely because its equation has been tested on analytic input. The complete main and supplementary figure inventory appears below. All rows have status \emph{not reproduced}; their data and plot commands are \emph{unavailable}, because no permitted empirical data-generation run could be specified. The JSON record retains these fields explicitly as null and records each stopped input assessment.

Published targets include the 54,319-event total, 38,103 best-located events, a completeness magnitude of 0.4 and $b=0.97$, northern ring depths of 2.1--2.5 km, deeper southwestern activity to 3.7 km, and 70 identified hybrid events. These are documentary scales, not this implementation's outputs. The source specification also distinguishes a deeper 4.8-km cluster outside the ring; therefore the 3.7-km conclusion must not be presented as a universal depth cutoff for all surrounding seismicity.

Catalog-count error, temporal alignment, spatial overlap, ring thickness, event-wise location error, focal-mechanism agreement, and hybrid-waveform similarity cannot be computed without permitted matched observations or results. Their absence is not zero error. FI errors and magnitude errors reported here are errors against analytic fixtures only. The generated figures do not test the reported FI range of $-3.32$ to $1.09$ or the paper's inferred temporal evolution.
