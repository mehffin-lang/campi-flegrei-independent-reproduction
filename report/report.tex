\begin{abstract}
The available description of Tan et al. (2025) concerns an observational earthquake catalog. It does not supply a coupled physical model from which the observed earthquake sequence can be generated. In this attempt, publisher access restrictions prevented retrieval of the full article and supplement, while the project instructions excluded the original observations and derived products. Consequently, no manuscript or supplementary panel is reproduced. I implement an independent C++ travel-time and hypocenter-location consistency example and save its numerical outputs before plotting them. Analytic path lengths, temporal invariance, station-order invariance, and a depth ambiguity provide numerical checks. These calculations establish limited numerical groundwork, not agreement with the published catalog. The panel inventory, complete workflow, and scientific reproduction remain incomplete.
\end{abstract}

\section{Why the requested coupled calculation is unresolved}
An empirical seismic catalog depends on measured signals and inference from those signals. Written descriptions of a detection method do not determine the particular observations. A complete forward physical model would require governing equations, constitutive laws, spatial material properties, forcing, and initial and boundary conditions. No such complete specification was recovered for the cited target. It would therefore be unjustified to choose a pressure source, fault law, or synthetic earthquake distribution and present its output as a recreation of Tan et al.

There are two distinct blockers. Access to readable full text and supplementary captions is required even to enumerate and classify the panels. Access to written methods alone would still not authorize the excluded original data, nor would it uniquely determine their measured values. The requested complete detection/association/relocation system and complete coupled physical model are not implemented here. The calculation below is an explicitly assumed limiting example relevant to location consistency; it is not a substitute for either system.

\section{Independent numerical example}
\subsection{Assumptions, equations, and units}
I assume a homogeneous, isotropic medium with constant wave speed $v$, direct straight paths, and exact arrival picks from one impulsive point event. Cartesian coordinates have units of meters, with $z$ positive downward. All receivers lie at $z=0$. There is no topography, refraction, scattering, attenuation, station correction, or clock error. The observable is an arrival time; no waveform amplitudes or fault mechanics are predicted.

For event location $\mathbf{x}$, receiver location $\mathbf{s}_i$, and origin time $t_0$, the path length and arrival are
\begin{equation}
 L_i=\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{s}_i\|,\qquad
 T_i=t_0+\frac{L_i}{v}.
\end{equation}
This follows by integrating $dt=ds/v$ along a straight path in a uniform medium. It is a first-principles limiting relation, not an equation recovered from the inaccessible Tan et al. methods. Given arrivals $a_i$ and a trial location, least squares eliminates origin time analytically:
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{t}_0(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N
 \left(a_i-\frac{L_i(\mathbf{x})}{v}\right),\quad
 R(\mathbf{x})=\left[\frac{1}{N}\sum_i
 \left(a_i-\widehat{t}_0(\mathbf{x})-\frac{L_i(\mathbf{x})}{v}\right)^2\right]^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
The code minimizes $R$, whose units are seconds. Eliminating the common time offset prevents an arbitrary reference time from changing the inferred position. Equal weights are assumed; there is no noise model or uncertainty estimate.

The synthetic parameters are $v=4000$ m/s, $\mathbf{x}=[370,-630,1730]$ m, and $t_0=10$ s. Twelve receiver positions are generated with
\begin{equation}
 \theta_j=\frac{2\pi j}{12},\quad r_j=1500+500(j\bmod 4)\ \mathrm{m},\quad
 \mathbf{s}_j=[r_j\cos\theta_j,r_j\sin\theta_j,0],\quad j=0,\ldots,11.
\end{equation}
These values are deliberately synthetic, not estimates for Campi Flegrei. The mixed radii provide path-length variation for depth sensitivity. No catalog-scale distribution is prescribed. The source time and position specify the initial event; the kinematic model has no temporal PDE initial state. It uses an unbounded uniform propagation medium and no reflective surface boundary condition. The receiver plane is solely a geometric constraint.

\subsection{Numerical search and its limits}
The initial search covers $x,y\in[-4000,4000]$ m and $z\in[0,4000]$ m on a 500 m grid. Each subsequent search is centered on the best point within one previous grid interval in each direction, clipped to the initial domain. The interval halves after each of 12 levels, ending at approximately 0.244 m. The search bounds are arbitrary validation-domain choices, not geological boundaries. The restriction $z\geq0$ selects the buried branch explicitly: surface receivers alone cannot distinguish $z$ from $-z$ in this model.

At each point, the implementation evaluates the analytic origin time and RMS residual in double precision. Work scales with the number of receivers times the number of trial points. Local refinement is efficient, but it does not prove a global minimum for arbitrary geometry or noisy observations. The retained best candidate makes residual nonincrease possible across refinement. Finer grid spacing is a consistency diagnostic, not a proof of convergence for the published workflow.

\section{Validation results}
The tests evaluate a zero path, a vertical path, and a 3--4--5 distance triangle against analytic times. They also recover a source deliberately located off the coarsest grid, shift all arrivals by 17 s, reverse the station order, and reflect source depth across the receiver plane. The reflected source must fit the exact data as well as the buried source; this test exposes the geometric ambiguity instead of hiding it. \section{Comparison and discrepancies}
No numerical agreement with a manuscript panel is established. The abstract's event-count scale, multiyear observation interval, and reported depth cutoff are potential future comparison targets, not inputs used to tune the validation example. The synthetic event at 1730 m is not evidence for the reported 3.7 km seismic limit. Its origin time of 10 s has no calendar meaning, and its synthetic receivers have no geographic registration. Consequently, catalog-count error, temporal alignment, hypocentral spatial error, ring thickness, and waveform similarity to the publication are undefined here. Assigning those metrics a value of zero would incorrectly imply agreement.

The independently computed validation errors quantify only an idealized inverse problem. Recreating the published spatial pattern would require admissible observations and a documented processing procedure; the present source-data prohibition prevents that route. Full text might still permit independent reproduction of specified synthetic method checks or redrawing conceptual schematics, but their existence and details cannot be asserted without the supplement. The current result is therefore an incomplete reproduction attempt with a runnable numerical example and documented evidence gaps.
