Campi Flegrei's unrest is recorded by ground displacement and seismicity. Tan et al. (2025) use a machine-learning-assisted earthquake catalog to resolve active structures and evaluate possible sources of unrest. Their study is observational: its central results describe the location, timing and character of earthquakes, rather than solutions of a coupled physical forward model. Here, the completed independent calculations are placed in the same scientific sequence. Published images are confined to the separate comparison PDF.

\section*{The current phase of unrest}
Figure 1B presents the independently processed historical displacement observations. The leveling series contains 128 tabulated entries from 1905--2009, and the daily RITE GNSS series extends through 2019. Relative to the March 1905 leveling observation, the leveling displacement ranges from $-1.266$ to $2.541$ m. The two observation series are joined by a constant offset estimated from their overlap. A separate December 2023 monitoring estimate is displayed as an endpoint; no continuous record is inferred across the missing recent daily observations.

The long record resolves alternating displacement episodes, but its incomplete recent portion cannot establish the detailed timing of deformation during the 2022--2025 seismic study. Panels 1A and 1C remain unreproduced under the existing panel assessment. Separately generated routine-catalog context is preserved in the documentation and is not presented as independent event detection.

\section*{Improving the earthquake catalog}
Independent PhaseNet inference and GaMMA association yield nine provisional events in the processed waveform sample. These are initial associations with an alternative starting velocity profile. They have not completed the velocity calibration and double-difference relocation required for a catalog comparable to Tan et al.'s result. Consequently, they cannot support an independent multiyear seismicity rate or depth--time distribution (Figure 2).

\section*{A clearer view of the current phase of unrest}
Figures 3 and 4 address the publication's main structural observations: the distribution of seismicity and the source mechanisms of larger events. The present independent work has no calibrated, relocated catalog or focal-mechanism solutions. It therefore cannot determine the depth range, continuity or temporal activation of the ring fault, resolve the central fault system, or establish its slip orientations. \section*{Implications for the physical process driving the unrest}
The reconstructed hybrid-template example in Figure S15C is an independently processed waveform observation. The limited template search produced four unconfirmed candidates; it does not establish recurring hybrid families or their evolution. Neither this result nor the initial associations can test the publication's inference about the presence or absence of upward seismic migration. The partial displacement history also does not distinguish magmatic and hydrothermal forcing. Such an interpretation would require the unresolved spatial and source constraints above.

\section*{New perspectives for improved seismic risk assessment}
Fault geometry and a calibrated magnitude distribution are needed to connect this analysis to seismic risk. No independently calibrated magnitude was obtained, and the magnitude-frequency analysis in Figure S8 remains unperformed. The current results therefore provide no revised estimate of event probabilities, damaging-event locations or source dimensions. Their supported contribution is restricted to the partial observational reconstructions shown here and in the supplement.
